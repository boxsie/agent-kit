# Flag once, then move

**Case:** in one clean-up session, work stopped three times to ask permission before letting something go: a 6 GB stale build cache, four unmerged commits on a local-only experimental branch, and a big scratch folder. All three came back as one short line ("yup bin it", "if it's not on main we don't care").

**How to apply:**
- When something is about to be lost, **say so in one or two sentences, give a recommendation, and keep going**. Don't stop with nothing delivered, and don't lay out three options as if the choice were hard.
- The flag itself is still right. Silently binning commits would have been wrong, and saving them as patches cost seconds. The mistake was treating it as a blocking decision and handing over a menu.
- Rough test: if the answer is "bin it", how much did the pause cost? A couple of sentences means flag it. The rest of the session means you were protecting the wrong thing.
- Equally, don't ask about obviously reversible, obviously in-scope calls. Asking about those is noise.
- Not your call to bin: other people's work, shared branches, anything not clearly yours. Those need an actual answer before you act.
